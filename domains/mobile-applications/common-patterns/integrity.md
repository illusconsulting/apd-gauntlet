# Mobile application common patterns — Integrity

These are illustrative templates, not all-inclusive. Use them to calibrate analytical style, severity assignment per the mobile application security rubric, and NIST/ATT&CK mapping habits. The specialist agent's analytical checklist still drives the actual analysis — this file calibrates how findings and capabilities should look once written.

## Common finding patterns

**Pattern: Untrusted inbound input — a deep-link or custom-scheme parameter, an intent extra, an IPC/content-provider payload, a push notification body, a WebView bridge call, an imported file, or an untrusted server response — flows into app logic with no validation, so the instruction-versus-data line is absent (the mobile mirror of indirect injection).**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when the unvalidated value reaches a backend-mutating call or a code/state-changing sink; the blast-radius modifier loads when a crafted link drives a write, and the device-trust modifier is *low* — a phishing link hits a stock device with no special preconditions)
- NIST: SI-10, SI-15, SC-7, AC-4
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the outbound-leakage face of the same entry points routes there), authenticity (verifying the inbound caller's identity — App Links domain verification — is its complement), and the server-side enforcement of any action the input drives routes to api-security / identity-security
- Detail: This is the inbound face of MASVS-PLATFORM-1 (IPC, deep links, exported components) and MASVS-PLATFORM-2 (the WebView bridge), with the client-side input-validation duty of MASVS-CODE-3, and it owns the MASWE platform-interaction and improper-input-validation weakness areas (CWE-20 Improper Input Validation, CWE-501 Trust Boundary Violation). MASTG covers it through the Android testing for unprotected exported components and deep-link handling and the iOS Universal Links / custom-scheme tests, plus the technique of fuzzing intent extras and scheme parameters. The ATT&CK Mobile relevant technique is delivery via a crafted link or deep link that drives in-app behavior. The structural control is a typed, validated boundary at every entry point in the `deep_link_and_ipc_entry_points` surface — reject-on-fail parsing, an allowlist of expected parameters, and the discipline that an inbound value is *data*, never an instruction or a trusted command; the missing server-side invariant is that any action the input names is re-authorized and re-validated on the backend, not performed because the client received the message.

**Pattern: A server-side control — an authorization check, a business rule, a price/entitlement/quantity computation, or a root/jailbreak verdict — is enforced ONLY on the client and trusted by the backend, so a patched or instrumented app sends the request the gate was meant to stop.**

- Severity: critical (the central-tension clause; the client-side-only-enforcement modifier pivots a device-local bypass into a backend write, and the scale-vector and blast-radius modifiers both load because every install can be patched)
- NIST: AC-3, AC-6, SI-10, AC-4
- Related concerns: authenticity (verifying the requester's identity is the complement of authorizing the request), non_repudiation (the backend record of the action); the server-side authorization and business-rule decision routes to api-security and the token/OAuth/OIDC and step-up protocol decision routes to identity-security — filed here as the mobile-origin critical with the "the client is not an authorization point" framing
- Detail: This is the MASVS-AUTH-1 enforcement face — authentication and authorization decisions that the rubric's central doctrine governs: client-side enforcement of a server-side control is not a control. The MASWE area is the client-side trust / improper-authorization weakness set (CWE-602 Client-Side Enforcement of Server-Side Security, CWE-639 Authorization Bypass). MASTG demonstrates it through the technique of patching the binary or hooking the decision with Frida to flip a gate and observing that the backend still accepts the resulting request, and through replaying a client-computed price or `is_premium` flag. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the use of a modified or instrumented client to issue requests outside the intended policy. The structural control is that the `mobile_backend_api_surface` re-derives every entitlement, price, and authorization server-side and never trusts a client-asserted security signal as an input; the RESILIENCE-1 verdict, if present, is an advisory risk input (see the RASP pattern below), never the gate itself.

**Pattern: A WebView with an exposed JavaScript bridge (`addJavascriptInterface` on Android, `WKScriptMessageHandler` / `WKUserContentController` on iOS) loads untrusted or mixed content, so web content crosses into native privilege.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when the bridge exposes file, storage, or arbitrary-native-method capability and the WebView can load attacker-influenced origins; the device-trust modifier is low because a stock device rendering hostile content is enough, and blast-radius widens to the `js_bridge_native_surface`)
- NIST: SC-18, SC-7, SI-10, AC-6
- Related concerns: confidentiality (a bridge that reads on-device data and ships it out is the exfiltration face), authenticity (origin verification of the loaded content); any privileged backend call the bridge ultimately makes routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-PLATFORM-2 (WebViews and the JavaScript bridge) and the MASWE WebView / bridge-injection weakness area (CWE-749 Exposed Dangerous Method, CWE-79 cross-context scripting into the bridge). MASTG covers it through the Android `addJavascriptInterface` and `setJavaScriptEnabled` / `setAllowFileAccess` tests and the iOS `WKWebView` message-handler and `loadFileURL` tests, plus the technique of injecting script into mixed or attacker-controlled content to invoke an exposed native method. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is delivery of malicious web content that abuses an over-broad app interface. The structural control is to minimize the bridge to a typed, allowlisted command set, disable file and universal access on the WebView, enforce an HTTPS-only origin allowlist for loaded content (no mixed content, no `file://` reaching the bridge), and treat any value arriving over the bridge as untrusted input subject to the validated-boundary pattern above; the server-side invariant remains that any backend action the bridge triggers is re-authorized server-side.

**Pattern: An exported component, an unverified deep link, or a custom URL scheme drives a state-changing action without server re-verification, so a co-resident app or a crafted link performs the action on the user's behalf.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when the action is a consequential backend transaction — funds movement, profile or credential mutation, a sharing/permission change; the blast-radius modifier loads and the device-trust modifier is low for the link-delivery case)
- NIST: AC-3, AC-4, SI-10, SC-7
- Related concerns: authenticity (App Links / Universal Links domain verification proves the link's origin), non_repudiation (an unverified inbound action is attributable to no one — see the consequential-action audit list); the server re-authorization of the named action routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-PLATFORM-1 (deep links, custom schemes, exported components, the `deep_link_and_ipc_entry_points` surface) and the MASWE unprotected-component / deep-link-hijack weakness area (CWE-926 Improper Export of Android Components, CWE-939 Improper Authorization in Handler for Custom URL Scheme). MASTG covers it through the Android exported-component and deep-link-handling tests and the iOS custom-scheme-versus-Universal-Link tests, including the technique of a co-resident app registering the same custom scheme to hijack the link. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is a malicious application intercepting or originating an intent/scheme to coerce action. The structural control is to remove unnecessary exports, require domain-verified Android App Links / iOS Universal Links (the custom scheme is hijackable, the verified link is not), validate every parameter, and — the finding of record — re-verify and re-authorize the named state change on the backend so a forged inbound message cannot satisfy the server-side authorization contract on its own.

**Pattern: The runtime-integrity / RASP signal (root, jailbreak, Frida/instrumentation, debugger, emulator) is absent, or is CLIENT-ENFORCED — the app blocks itself locally — rather than shipped to the server as an advisory risk input.**

- Severity: medium when merely absent or locally enforced (it is advisory defense-in-depth, not a control; the device-trust modifier is maximal — the bypass needs a rooted device with Frida or Magisk); escalates to critical under the client-side-enforcement clause if the backend actually consumes the client's self-asserted verdict as an authorization gate
- NIST: SI-4, SI-7, RA-3, AU-6
- Related concerns: authenticity (hardware attestation — Play Integrity / App Attest — is the *verifiable* floor this signal only approximates; see that pattern in authenticity.md), non_repudiation (the verdict must be recorded server-side as a security-relevant event); consumption of the risk signal in a step-up decision routes to identity-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-RESILIENCE-1 (the runtime-integrity / reverse-engineering-resistance detection set) and the MASWE anti-tampering / runtime-detection weakness area; the rubric files it as Medium precisely because a `rooted_device_attacker_with_frida` defeats any on-device check. MASTG covers it through the Android root-detection and anti-debugging tests and the iOS jailbreak-detection tests, plus the technique of hooking or patching the detection routine with Frida to force a clean verdict. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the use of device compromise (rooting/jailbreak) and runtime instrumentation to evade defenses. The structural control is that the detection result is *shipped to and evaluated by the backend* as one input to a server-side risk decision (raise step-up, restrict scope, flag for review), never a client-side block whose only record sits on the compromised device; the genuine integrity floor is hardware attestation, which Authenticity owns.

**Pattern: No binary anti-tamper / repackaging / re-sign detection exists, and there is no compensating server-side attestation, so a modified, repackaged, or re-signed build is indistinguishable to the backend from the genuine app.**

- Severity: medium absent a server-trusted decision behind it (a tamper signal with no server consumer is advisory); escalates if a client-trusted control depends on binary integrity, or pairs with a missing attestation floor to defeat the only proof the build is the one that was shipped
- NIST: SI-7, CM-5, SR-11, RA-3
- Related concerns: authenticity (the `app_signing_identity` and signed-build provenance, plus Play Integrity / App Attest, are the verifiable proof of a genuine binary — this routes to authenticity.md), immutability (the signed-build / provenance record must be immutable per the immutability classes); the backend verification of the attestation verdict routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-RESILIENCE-3 (app-integrity / anti-repackaging detection) and the MASWE repackaging / code-integrity weakness area (CWE-494 Download of Code Without Integrity Check for the side-load case). MASTG covers it through the Android signature-verification (`PackageManager` signing-certificate) and anti-tamper tests and the iOS code-signing / re-sign tests, plus the technique of unpacking, modifying, and re-signing an APK or IPA. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the repackaging or distribution of a trojanized application. On-device tamper checks raise the `reverse_engineer_with_ipa_apk` attacker's cost but are themselves patchable; the structural control — and finding of record — is a server-side attestation invariant (Play Integrity / App Attest verifying the genuine, untampered package signature and integrity verdict at the backend) so the `mobile_backend_api_surface` distinguishes a genuine build from a repackaged one regardless of what the client claims.

**Pattern: A vulnerable or poisoned in-process third-party component alters app behavior — the alteration face of the supply chain — because a bundled SDK or dependency runs with the app's full privilege and is neither pinned, provenance-verified, nor capability-scoped.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical for a malicious or backdoored SDK shipped to the install base — the backdoored-SDK scale-vector reaches every user's on-device data and tokens with one update; high for a known-vulnerable dependency whose flaw alters behavior)
- NIST: SR-3, SR-4, SR-11, SI-7, CM-7
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the in-process exfiltration face of the same `malicious_in_process_sdk` position), non_repudiation (the SDK-collection / data-flow record per the immutability classes); any backend the SDK calls routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-CODE-2 (third-party components / dependency security) and the MASWE supply-chain / dependency weakness area (CWE-1395 Dependency on Vulnerable Third-Party Component, CWE-506 Embedded Malicious Code). MASTG covers it through the dependency-inventory and SBOM testing and the technique of inspecting bundled frameworks/AARs and their network and reflection behavior. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the supply-chain compromise of an in-app library and the abuse of broad in-process capability. The structural control is an SBOM with version pinning and provenance verification across the `app_to_third_party_sdk` boundary, capability-scoping and egress review of each SDK, and a forced-update / kill-switch path (MASVS-CODE-4) to retire a compromised dependency fleet-wide; the server-side invariant is that no SDK-originated request is trusted more than any other client request — the backend still re-authorizes it. This SBOM-plus-provenance posture is also the software-assurance evidence NIST SP 800-163r1's app-vetting process evaluates — the bundled-component inventory and its provenance are inputs to the app-approval decision and to re-vetting when a dependency changes, so an unattested dependency set fails the organizational vetting gate as well as the runtime trust check.

**Pattern: In-transit payload tampering is possible because integrity is not protected independently of TLS on a high-value channel — a downgraded, stripped, or MitM-intercepted session can alter the request or response body.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when validation/cert-trust is disabled or trust-all on a credential- or PII-bearing channel so a stock-device MitM rewrites payloads at scale; high when default TLS holds but there is no pinning against a `network_mitm` with a user-installed CA)
- NIST: SC-8, SC-8(1), SC-13, SC-23
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the eavesdropping face of the same channel), authenticity (certificate / public-key pinning verifies the server identity — the NETWORK-2 pinning facet routes there); the server-side validation of the received payload routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-NETWORK-1 (secure-channel / TLS) on its tamper face and the MASWE insecure-communication weakness area (CWE-319 Cleartext Transmission, CWE-295 Improper Certificate Validation). MASTG covers it through the Android Network Security Config and cleartext-traffic tests and the iOS App Transport Security tests, plus the technique of an intercepting proxy with a user-installed CA modifying request and response bodies. The ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the network interception / manipulation of application traffic. The structural control is TLS with strict certificate validation enforced by Network Security Config (Android) and App Transport Security (iOS) — never a trust-all override — and, on a high-value channel, payload integrity that does not rest on transport alone plus certificate/public-key pinning (the NETWORK-2 / Authenticity facet); the server-side invariant is that the backend independently validates and re-authorizes every received payload rather than trusting that transport preserved it.

**Pattern: A hybrid app applies an over-the-air JavaScript/bundle code-push update without verifying a bundle signature anchored to the developer's key, so a tampered or attacker-pushed bundle executes app logic across the install base outside store review.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when the OTA bundle reaches a backend-mutating or code-exec sink and the channel is unauthenticated; the scale-vector dominates — one malicious bundle hot-updates the whole fleet outside the store-review gate — and the device-trust modifier is *low* because a `network_mitm` on the update fetch or a `malicious_ota_update` from a compromised code-push endpoint hits stock devices)
- NIST: SI-7, SR-4, SR-11, CM-3, SC-8(1)
- Related concerns: authenticity (the bundle-signing identity and provenance that make the update verifiable — see authenticity.md), ephemeral (the OTA channel must be governed by the same minimum-bundle-version sunset as the store binary — see ephemeral.md), confidentiality (the bundle is a reverse-engineering / secret-extraction surface like the native binary); the server-side bundle-signing and provenance service routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-CODE-2 (third-party / update integrity) intersecting MASVS-RESILIENCE-3 (code / anti-tamper) for React Native / Flutter / Capacitor apps using CodePush, EAS Update, or a homegrown OTA channel, in the MASWE code-integrity / unverified-update area with CWE-494 Download of Code Without Integrity Check. MASTG resilience and code-quality testing inspects whether the update is signature-verified before it is applied; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the delivery of malicious code via an application update. The structural control is that the client verifies a bundle signature chained to the developer's signing key (plus a freshness / version check) before executing any OTA bundle, the update is fetched over a pinned channel, and the bundle's provenance is recorded immutably like the store build; the missing server-side invariant — as with the store binary — is that no OTA-delivered code is trusted to enforce a server-side control, and the backend re-authorizes every action regardless of which bundle version issued it.

**Pattern: The tech plan describes "input validation," "hardening," or "tamper protection" generically, without specifying the validated entry-point contract, where server-side re-authorization happens, or how the runtime-integrity / attestation signal is consumed.**

- Disposition: blocked or uncertainty
- prerequisite_evidence: "Mobile integrity specification — the enumerated inbound entry points (deep links, App Links/Universal Links, custom schemes, exported components, IPC, push, WebView bridge, imported files) with their per-parameter validation and reject-on-fail behavior; the server-side authorization and business-rule re-verification contract for every action the client merely invokes (the api-security / identity-security boundary); the WebView bridge command allowlist and origin allowlist; and whether the RESILIENCE-1 runtime-integrity verdict and RESILIENCE-3 tamper/attestation signal are shipped to and consumed by the backend as an advisory risk input or are client-enforced"

## Common capability patterns

**Pattern: Deterministic server-side enforcement of every authorization and business rule, with the client treated as untrusted — no entitlement, price, quantity, or security verdict is accepted from the client as an authorization input.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan names the backend as the sole authorization point and lists which decisions are re-derived server-side; implemented when the `mobile_backend_api_surface` is shown re-checking object- and function-level authorization and recomputing prices/entitlements per request (routing to api-security / identity-security for the server-side rule); tested when a patched or Frida-hooked client demonstrably fails to obtain an unauthorized backend write; operationalized when fail-closed denial is evidenced and client-asserted security signals are confirmed to carry zero authorization weight.

**Pattern: A typed, validated boundary at every inbound entry point, plus Android App Links / iOS Universal Links domain verification, so untrusted input is data and every link's origin is provable.** Maturity: designed when every entry point in the `deep_link_and_ipc_entry_points` surface is enumerated with its expected parameter schema; implemented when reject-on-fail validation and an allowlist are in place and custom schemes are replaced or backed by domain-verified App Links / Universal Links; tested when fuzzing intent extras, scheme parameters, and bridge payloads cannot drive an unintended state change; operationalized when new entry points cannot ship without passing the boundary contract and the App Links `assetlinks.json` / `apple-app-site-association` verification is monitored.

**Pattern: Runtime-integrity verdicts (root/jailbreak/Frida/debugger/emulator) are shipped to AND consumed by the server as a risk input, never client-blocking.** Maturity: designed when the plan states the verdict is advisory and names the server-side risk decision it feeds (step-up, scope restriction, review); implemented when the detection runs and the verdict reaches the backend over an authenticated channel; tested when a clean-verdict forge on a rooted device is shown to *not* grant authorization on its own (the backend treats it as one signal among several, with hardware attestation as the floor); operationalized when verdicts are recorded server-side as security events per the consequential-action list and feed an active risk-scoring or step-up decision rather than sitting unread.

**Pattern: Anti-repackaging and re-sign detection coupled to server-side attestation, so a tampered build is distinguishable to the backend (cross-cuts Authenticity).** Maturity: designed when the plan pairs on-device tamper detection with a server-verified Play Integrity / App Attest invariant rather than relying on the client check alone; implemented when the backend verifies the attestation verdict and the genuine package signature server-side and the signed-build provenance record is captured (immutably, per the immutability classes); tested when a repackaged or re-signed build is shown to fail backend attestation even with on-device checks patched out; operationalized when attestation freshness is enforced (no replay of a stale verdict), failures are recorded as security events, and the forced-update / kill-switch path can retire a compromised build fleet-wide.
