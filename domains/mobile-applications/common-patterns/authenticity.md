# Mobile application common patterns — Authenticity

These are illustrative templates, not all-inclusive. Use them to calibrate analytical style, severity assignment per the mobile application security rubric, and NIST/ATT&CK mapping habits. The specialist agent's analytical checklist still drives the actual analysis — this file calibrates how findings and capabilities should look once written, not what to look for.

The eight MASVS categories are cross-cutting, not goals. Authenticity owns cryptographically *verifiable* client identity — the doctrine anchor of this pack. It holds hardware attestation (MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 Play Integrity / App Attest / key attestation) as the verifiable proof of a genuine app on a genuine device that makes root/Frida heuristics moot, certificate / public-key pinning (MASVS-NETWORK-2) as verification of the *server's* identity, biometric-as-key-release (MASVS-AUTH-3 with MASVS-CRYPTO-2) as the only authenticity-bearing form of local authentication, app-signing and build provenance (MASVS-RESILIENCE-3 with MASVS-CODE-1) as proof the binary is the one that was shipped, AAL and OAuth PKCE on the mobile public client (MASVS-AUTH-1/3), SDK and dependency provenance (MASVS-CODE-2), and deep-link / IPC caller identity and App Links / Universal Links domain verification (MASVS-PLATFORM-1). Client-side enforcement of a server-side control is not a control: where an identity check runs only on the adversary-controlled device, it raises attacker cost but is bypassable on a rooted or Frida-instrumented client; score it as defense-in-depth and make the finding of record the missing server-side invariant — the identity proof the backend must verify, not the client must assert. This pack is client-focused and composes with the api-security and identity-security packs in a multi-domain run; route server-side authz and business-rule enforcement to api-security and token-issuance / OAuth / OIDC protocol decisions to identity-security via the Related concerns line.

## Common finding patterns

**Pattern: No hardware attestation (Play Integrity / App Attest / key attestation) — the backend has no cryptographic proof that a request came from a genuine, untampered app running on a genuine, uncompromised device.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when consequential `mobile_backend_api_surface` writes — funds movement, credential mutation — are accepted with no genuine-client proof and the only existing defenses are client-side root/Frida heuristics that the `rooted_device_attacker_with_frida` position defeats; the scale-vector loads because a single forged or emulated client multiplies across the install base, and this is the *verifiable* answer that makes every device-trust-assumption argument moot)
- NIST: IA-9, SI-7, SI-7(6), SC-23, RA-3
- Related concerns: integrity (the RESILIENCE-1 root/jailbreak/Frida verdict is the *advisory* signal this attestation is the verifiable floor for — see the RASP pattern in integrity.md); non_repudiation (the attestation-verification outcome is a consequential-action audit event and an immutable attestation-verification record); the protocol for issuing a token *bound* to the attestation result routes to identity-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 (hardware-backed attestation / device-and-app integrity) and the MASWE area for missing device/app attestation, the verifiable counterpart to the RESILIENCE-1 runtime-detection weakness set. The crown jewel is `attestation_tokens_and_jwts` across the `device_to_attestation_authority` boundary. MASTG covers it through the attestation knowledge and best-practice content on Play Integrity (Android) and DCAppAttest / App Attest (iOS) and the technique of verifying the integrity verdict and certificate chain server-side; the `rooted_device_attacker_with_frida` and `reverse_engineer_with_ipa_apk` positions are exactly what it defeats, and the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the use of a compromised or emulated runtime to evade defenses (CWE-1242 Inclusion of Undocumented Features / weak attestation). The structural control — and finding of record — is that the backend requests a fresh attestation, verifies the Google / Apple-signed verdict and key-attestation chain server-side, and binds a high-value action or a session token to that verdict; client-side root detection is not the proof, the server-verified attestation is. Do not score the bypassability of the on-device heuristic; score the consequence of the backend having no cryptographic genuine-client proof at all.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-RESILIENCE-4; MASWE-0099

**Pattern: No certificate or public-key pinning on a high-value channel — the server's identity is unverified beyond default CA trust, so a user-installed MitM CA or a hostile-network adversary can impersonate the backend.**

- Severity: high (the `network_mitm` position with a user-installed CA or a compromised CA impersonates the backend and harvests `stored_access_token` / `profile_pii` or rewrites responses; high rather than critical because stock-device default TLS still holds against the casual on-path case with no installed CA, but the device-trust assumption is *low* for the installed-CA case and the blast radius pivots to the backend; drops toward the operational-hygiene clause when pinning is present but the rotation/backup-pin strategy is undocumented)
- NIST: SC-8, SC-8(1), SC-23, IA-9, SC-17
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the eavesdropping face of the same unverified channel routes to confidentiality.md), integrity (a MitM that impersonates the server also rewrites the payloads the client trusts — routes to integrity.md); the server-side refusal to accept downgraded sessions and the mTLS / token-binding protocol route to api-security and identity-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-NETWORK-2 (server-identity verification / pinning) and the MASWE area for improper certificate / server-identity validation, with CWE-295 Improper Certificate Validation. The pinned set is the `pinning_public_key_set` from the data taxonomy — integrity-critical, and a wrong or unrotatable pin is its own finding. MASTG covers it through the pinning knowledge and best-practice content and the technique of an intercepting proxy with a user-installed CA on both platforms, checking the Android Network Security Config `<pin-set>` and the iOS pinning implementation; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is adversary-in-the-middle on the network path. The structural control is certificate or, preferably, public-key (SPKI) pinning of the leaf or intermediate on the `client_to_backend_api` boundary for high-value channels — enforced via the Android Network Security Config or a vetted pinning library, and on iOS via `URLSession` delegate validation or App Transport Security pin configuration — with at least one backup pin and a documented rotation strategy so a key roll does not brick the fleet; a pin-validation *failure* is itself a security-relevant event reported server-side. Pinning verifies who the server is; it does not relieve the backend of independently re-authorizing every request.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-NETWORK-2; MASWE-0052

**Pattern: Biometric or device-credential local authentication gates only the UI flow, not the release of a Keystore / Secure Enclave key — so a patched or Frida-hooked client performs the protected action without any factor ever being presented.**

- Severity: critical (the client-side-only-enforcement modifier governs: a UI-only `BiometricPrompt` / `LAContext` gate is a screen, not a control, and the `rooted_device_attacker_with_frida` position hooks `canEvaluatePolicy` / the success callback to skip it; when the gated action is a consequential backend transaction with no server re-authentication, a device-local bypass becomes a backend write — the finding of record is the missing server-side step-up, not the bypassable prompt)
- NIST: IA-2, IA-2(1), IA-5, IA-5(2), SC-12
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the `biometric_gated_secrets` crown jewel — the *release* of the key, not the prompt, is the sensitive event, and the at-rest key protection routes to confidentiality.md), non_repudiation (the key-release event, not the biometric prompt, is the audit anchor per the consequential-action list); the server-side step-up / re-authentication protocol for the high-value action routes to identity-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-AUTH-3 (local authentication) compounded by MASVS-CRYPTO-2 (key protection), and the MASWE area for local-authentication that is not cryptographically bound to a key — the central rubric clause that a privileged action gated only by a client-side local-auth check with no server re-authentication is critical. MASTG covers it through the local-authentication knowledge and best-practice content and the technique of hooking the biometric callback or patching the success branch with Frida to reach the action with no factor presented; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the bypass of a local security control on a compromised device (CWE-287 Improper Authentication, CWE-603 Use of Client-Side Authentication). The structural control is that the biometric *releases* a hardware-bound key — Android `setUserAuthenticationRequired(true)` (ideally `setIsStrongBoxBacked(true)`) so the key is usable only after a fresh `BiometricPrompt`, or an iOS Keychain item / Secure Enclave key under a `kSecAccessControl` with `.biometryCurrentSet` evaluated through `LAContext` — so a bypassed prompt yields no usable key and produces no valid cryptographic proof; and the missing server-side invariant is that the consequential action requires a server-verified step-up assertion (a signature made with the biometric-released key, or a backend re-authentication), never a client-asserted "biometric passed" flag.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-AUTH-3, MASVS-CRYPTO-2; MASWE-0042

**Pattern: App-signing and build provenance are unverified by the backend — a repackaged, re-signed, or side-loaded build is indistinguishable to the server from the genuine app.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when a trojanized build can reach users — side-load, third-party store, or compromised distribution — and the backend accepts its requests as genuine; the `reverse_engineer_with_ipa_apk` position unpacks, modifies, re-signs, and redistributes, and on-device signature checks are themselves patchable on the `rooted_device_attacker_with_frida` position, so the device-trust argument cannot resolve it — only a server-side proof can)
- NIST: SI-7, SI-7(6), CM-5, SR-4, SR-11
- Related concerns: integrity (the anti-repackaging / re-sign detection face is owned by the RESILIENCE-3 pattern in integrity.md — this file owns the *verifiable provenance* it must be paired with), immutability (the signed-build / app-signing provenance record — build digest, signing identity, SBOM, release version — must be immutable per the immutability classes), non_repudiation (repackaging attribution depends on that provenance record); backend verification of the package signature routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-RESILIENCE-3 (app integrity / anti-repackaging) on its *verifiable* face, paired with MASVS-CODE-1 (the build / platform configuration that fixes signing), and the MASWE area for missing code-integrity / repackaging detection, with CWE-494 Download of Code Without Integrity Check for the side-load case. The `app_signing_identity` crown jewel is at stake. MASTG covers it through the app-integrity knowledge content and the Android signature-verification technique (`PackageManager` signing-certificate inspection) and the iOS code-signing / re-sign tests, plus the unpack-modify-re-sign technique (apktool, codesign); the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the repackaging and distribution of a trojanized application. On-device signature self-checks raise the attacker's cost but are bypassable; the structural control is server-side: the backend verifies, via hardware attestation (Play Integrity's `appRecognitionVerdict` / App Attest binding the request to the genuine bundle and signing identity), that the requesting binary is the one that was shipped, and the immutable signed-build provenance record (build digest, signing certificate, SBOM, version) is the basis for repackaging attribution and the forced-update / kill-switch decision. The missing server-side invariant is that a genuine build is distinguishable from a repackaged one at the backend, regardless of what the client claims. In a managed/enterprise distribution context this signed-build provenance is also the evidentiary baseline NIST SP 800-163r1's app-vetting process consumes — the app-approval decision and any re-vetting on update are anchored to a verifiable build identity, so unverifiable provenance breaks both the runtime trust decision and the organizational vetting record (which Non-Repudiation and Immutability own). For hybrid apps that ship a JavaScript/Dart bundle and hot-update it over the air (CodePush, EAS Update), the same provenance discipline extends to the OTA bundle: the client must verify a bundle signature chained to the developer's key before executing an update, because an OTA channel that applies an unsigned bundle is an app-signing bypass reaching the install base outside store review (see the OTA pattern in integrity.md).
- MAS mapping: MASVS-RESILIENCE-3, MASVS-CODE-1; MASWE-0097

**Pattern: The user or operator is authenticated below the required AAL for the action the mobile client commands, and/or the OAuth public client performs authorization without PKCE — so an authorization-code interception or a weak-factor compromise yields the user's session.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when a low-AAL session or a non-PKCE code interception grants a consequential backend transaction at scale; the OAuth public-client case loads the device-trust-low modifier because authorization-code interception by a `co_resident_malicious_app` or `phishing_deep_link_or_ipc_caller` registering the redirect scheme needs no rooted device; the AAL-shortfall case is the central rubric clause where a step-up is gated client-side only)
- NIST: IA-2, IA-2(1), IA-2(2), IA-2(8), IA-5(2)
- Related concerns: ephemeral (authenticator and step-up token lifetime per AAL — couples to identity-security), non_repudiation (per-AAL attribution of the sign-in and step-up events per the consequential-action list); the OAuth / OIDC protocol decision (PKCE, redirect-URI binding, code lifetime) and the AAL target itself route to identity-security — filed here as the mobile-origin authenticity finding
- Detail: This is MASVS-AUTH-1 (authentication of the user) and MASVS-AUTH-3 (the step-up / sensitive-transaction event), with the MASWE area for weak authentication / insecure OAuth on a public client, CWE-287 Improper Authentication, and CWE-307-adjacent weak-factor concerns. A mobile app is an OAuth *public client* — it holds no client secret (a `client_id` is public by design per the data taxonomy out-of-scope note), so PKCE (RFC 7636) is mandatory to bind the authorization code to the requesting app and defeat code interception. MASTG covers it through the authentication-architecture knowledge content and the technique of inspecting the OAuth flow for `code_challenge` / `code_verifier` and a claimed `https` App Link / Universal Link redirect rather than a hijackable custom scheme; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the theft of an application access token via interception of the authorization response. The structural control is PKCE on every mobile authorization-code flow, a redirect bound to a domain-verified App Link / Universal Link (not a custom scheme a co-resident app can claim — see the deep-link pattern below), and an explicit AAL target (NIST SP 800-63B AAL2/AAL3 by action blast radius) with the *server* enforcing step-up for consequential actions; the missing server-side invariant is that the backend verifies the AAL of the action it performs rather than trusting a client-asserted "step-up passed" flag.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-AUTH-1, MASVS-AUTH-3; MASWE-0040

**Pattern: Third-party SDK and dependency provenance is unattested — no SBOM or SLSA-style build provenance over the bundled components, so a swapped, backdoored, or typosquatted in-process library is undetectable.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical for a malicious or backdoored SDK shipped to the install base — the `malicious_in_process_sdk` scale-vector reaches every user's `cached_pii_phi_on_device`, `stored_access_token`, and key surface with one update, running with the app's full privilege; high for an unattested-but-not-yet-malicious dependency where the absent provenance means a future swap is invisible)
- NIST: SR-3, SR-4, SR-4(3), SR-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the in-process exfiltration face of the same `malicious_in_process_sdk` position routes to confidentiality.md), integrity (the behavior-alteration face routes to the supply-chain pattern in integrity.md), non_repudiation (the SDK-version / collection / data-flow record per the immutability classes — a new SDK build is a new in-process trust decision); any backend the SDK calls routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-CODE-2 (third-party components / dependency provenance) and the MASWE supply-chain / dependency weakness area, with CWE-1395 Dependency on Vulnerable Third-Party Component and CWE-494 Download of Code Without Integrity Check. The boundary is `app_to_third_party_sdk`. MASTG covers it through the dependency-inventory and SBOM knowledge / best-practice content and the technique of inspecting bundled AARs / frameworks / SPM-CocoaPods-Gradle dependencies and their declared versions against an attestation; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is the supply-chain compromise of an in-app library. The structural control is an SBOM (CycloneDX / SPDX) with version pinning and SLSA-style build provenance over every bundled component, verified at build time and re-verified on any dependency change, plus capability scoping and egress review of each SDK across the `app_to_third_party_sdk` boundary; because the SDK runs in-process with the app's privilege, provenance is the authenticity layer (proving the component is the one that was vetted) and the missing server-side invariant is that no SDK-originated request is trusted more than any other client request.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-CODE-2; MASWE-0079

**Pattern: Deep-link / IPC caller identity is unverified — App Links / Universal Links domain verification is absent so a custom scheme is hijackable, and an inbound intent / IPC payload carries no proof of who sent it.**

- Severity: high to critical (critical when an unverified inbound link or IPC call drives a consequential backend transaction with no server re-authorization; the device-trust modifier is *low* — a `phishing_deep_link_or_ipc_caller` link hits a stock device, and a `co_resident_malicious_app` registering the same custom scheme intercepts the link or originates the action with no rooted device required)
- NIST: IA-9, AC-3, AC-4, SC-7, AC-6
- Related concerns: integrity (the parameter-validation / instruction-versus-data face of the same `deep_link_and_ipc_entry_points` surface is owned by integrity.md — this file owns the *caller-identity* face), non_repudiation (an unverified inbound action is attributable to no one, and the link's domain-verification status is part of the audit record per the consequential-action list); the server re-authorization of any action the link names routes to api-security
- Detail: This is MASVS-PLATFORM-1 (IPC, deep links, exported components — the caller-identity face) and the MASWE area for improper deep-link / IPC handling and unprotected components, with CWE-939 Improper Authorization in Handler for Custom URL Scheme and CWE-926 Improper Export of Android Components. The surface is `deep_link_and_ipc_entry_points` across the `deep_link_ipc_entry_boundary`. MASTG covers it through the Android App Links verification and deep-link knowledge content and the iOS Universal Links / custom-scheme tests, plus the technique of a co-resident app registering a duplicate custom scheme to hijack the link or sending an unauthenticated intent to an exported component; the ATT&CK Mobile grounding is a malicious application intercepting or originating an intent / scheme to coerce action. The structural control is to prefer domain-verified Android App Links (`autoVerify="true"` with a hosted `assetlinks.json`) and iOS Universal Links (a hosted `apple-app-site-association`) over custom schemes — the verified link's origin is provable, the custom scheme's is not — to verify the calling package / IPC sender identity where the platform allows it (`Binder.getCallingUid` / signature-level permissions on Android), to mark non-public components `android:exported="false"`, and — the finding of record — to re-authorize on the backend any action an inbound message names, so a forged inbound caller cannot satisfy the server-side authorization contract on its own.
- MAS mapping: MASVS-PLATFORM-1; MASWE-0072

**Pattern: Tech plan asserts the app is "attested," "pinned," or "biometrically protected" generically without specifying the attestation provider and server-side verification, the pinned key set and rotation strategy, what the biometric actually releases, or the OAuth/AAL configuration.**

- Disposition: blocked or uncertainty
- prerequisite_evidence: "Mobile authenticity specification — the hardware-attestation provider (Play Integrity / App Attest / key attestation), what is attested, and where the verdict and certificate chain are verified server-side and bound to which action or token; the certificate/public-key pinning configuration (which channels, leaf vs intermediate vs SPKI, the backup-pin and rotation strategy, and the pin-failure reporting path); whether biometric/local auth gates the UI only or *releases* a hardware-bound Keystore/Secure Enclave key (the `setUserAuthenticationRequired` / `kSecAccessControl` evidence) and what server-side step-up the consequential action requires; the OAuth flow on the public client (PKCE `code_challenge` presence, the redirect binding to a domain-verified App Link / Universal Link, code lifetime) and the explicit AAL target per action; the SBOM / provenance over bundled SDKs with each SDK's version pin; and the App Links / Universal Links domain-verification status and caller-identity verification for each inbound deep-link / IPC entry point"

## Common capability patterns

**Pattern: Hardware attestation (Play Integrity / App Attest) is verified server-side and consumed as a write-authorization input, not as a client-side block.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan names the attestation provider and states that a fresh verdict gates which actions and is verified at the backend; implemented when the app requests Play Integrity / App Attest at the right moments and the backend verifies the Google / Apple-signed verdict and the key-attestation chain server-side over the `device_to_attestation_authority` boundary; tested when a rooted / emulated / repackaged client is shown to *fail* the server-side verdict and is denied the high-value action even with on-device root-detection patched out, and when a replayed stale verdict is rejected for freshness; operationalized when the attestation-verification outcome is recorded server-side as a consequential-action event and an immutable attestation-verification record, freshness/nonce binding is enforced, and the verdict is bound to the issued session token or per-action step-up rather than logged and ignored. MAS mapping: MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 (control satisfied).

**Pattern: Certificate or public-key (SPKI) pinning on high-value channels with a documented rotation and backup-pin strategy.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan names which channels are pinned, the pin granularity (leaf / intermediate / SPKI), and that at least one backup pin and a rotation plan exist; implemented when the Android Network Security Config `<pin-set>` or a vetted pinning library and the iOS `URLSession`-delegate / ATS pinning are in place on the `client_to_backend_api` boundary with the `pinning_public_key_set` enumerated and a backup pin loaded; tested when an intercepting proxy with a user-installed CA fails to read or rewrite the pinned channel against a `network_mitm` position, and a simulated key roll to the backup pin does not brick the client; operationalized when pin rotation is exercised on a schedule ahead of certificate expiry, a pin-validation failure is reported server-side as a security event, and the pin configuration is enforced in CI against drift. MAS mapping: MASVS-NETWORK-2 (control satisfied).

**Pattern: Biometric-gated Keystore / Secure Enclave key RELEASE (not a UI gate) for sensitive transactions, with server-verified step-up.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan states the biometric *releases a hardware-bound key* used to produce a server-verifiable assertion, not merely shows a prompt, and names the consequential actions it guards; implemented when code shows an Android key created with `setUserAuthenticationRequired(true)` (ideally `setIsStrongBoxBacked(true)`) usable only after a fresh `BiometricPrompt`, or an iOS Keychain item / Secure Enclave key under a `kSecAccessControl` with `.biometryCurrentSet` evaluated through `LAContext`, and the released key signs the action; tested when a Frida hook of the biometric success callback yields no usable key and no valid server assertion (the bypass produces nothing the backend accepts), and a biometric-enrollment change is shown to invalidate the key; operationalized when the key-release event (not the prompt) is the server-side audit anchor per the consequential-action list, biometric-enrollment-change invalidation is wired up, and the backend independently verifies the step-up assertion rather than trusting a client flag. MAS mapping: MASVS-AUTH-3, MASVS-CRYPTO-2 (control satisfied).

**Pattern: PKCE on the mobile public client (protocol routed to identity-security) plus an SBOM and build provenance over bundled SDKs.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan states the authorization-code flow uses PKCE with a redirect bound to a domain-verified App Link / Universal Link, names the AAL target per action, and commits to an SBOM with provenance over bundled components; implemented when the flow shows a `code_challenge` / `code_verifier` and a claimed `https` redirect (no hijackable custom scheme) and the build emits a CycloneDX / SPDX SBOM with SLSA-style provenance and pinned SDK versions (the protocol details route to identity-security, the SBOM to MASVS-CODE-2); tested when an authorization-code interception by a co-resident app or a duplicate-scheme registration fails without the matching verifier, and a swapped or unpinned dependency is caught by provenance verification at build time; operationalized when PKCE and the verified redirect are enforced for every flow, the AAL is verified server-side for consequential actions, and SBOM / provenance verification runs on every dependency change with a forced-update / kill-switch path (MASVS-CODE-4) to retire a compromised component fleet-wide. MAS mapping: MASVS-AUTH-1, MASVS-CODE-2 (control satisfied).
