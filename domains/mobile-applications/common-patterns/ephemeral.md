# Mobile application common patterns — Ephemeral

These are illustrative templates, not all-inclusive. Use them to calibrate analytical style, severity assignment per the mobile application security rubric, and NIST/ATT&CK mapping habits. The specialist agent's analytical checklist still drives the actual analysis — this file calibrates how findings and capabilities should look once written. Ephemeral owns the lifetime facet on the client: on-device token/session lifetime and server revocability, secret and cache wipe on logout/deauth/biometric-change, Keystore/Keychain key rotation and invalidation, attestation-verdict freshness, the forced-version sunset (the Ephemeral-owned face of CODE-4), and pinned-trust-anchor expiry — not at-rest secret protection (Confidentiality) or identity strength (Authenticity). The eight MASVS categories are cross-cutting, not goals; each goal's patterns own their facet. And on an adversary-controlled client, client-side enforcement of a server-side control is not a control — a lifetime check that runs only on the device is defense-in-depth, and the finding of record is the missing server-side invariant.

## Common finding patterns

**Pattern: A long-lived access or refresh token is persisted on the device with no server-side revocation, no rotation, and no device-binding.**

- Severity: high (blast-radius and reversibility load: an extracted token is replayable for its full lifetime with no recovery path, and absent device-binding it is replayable off-device by any holder; escalates toward critical when the token's scope reaches a consequential backend write)
- NIST: IA-5, IA-5(13), AC-12, SC-12, AU-9
- Related concerns: Authenticity (the token-presentation and device-binding proof); routes the token-issuance pipeline, refresh-rotation policy, and OAuth/OIDC lifetime decisions to identity-security, and the server-side revocation endpoint and introspection check to api-security
- Detail: This is the lifetime face of MASVS-AUTH-2 (the stolen-/extracted-token area of MASWE under AUTH), distinct from the at-rest storage protection that MASVS-STORAGE-1 owns. A refresh token cached in the Keychain or Android Keystore is still replayable if it never expires and the backend cannot revoke it; the relevant MASTG work is the testing of session-timeout, token-lifetime, and logout-invalidation behavior under the AUTH test group, and key-attestation testing where device-binding is claimed. Closest CWE is CWE-613 Insufficient Session Expiration. The ATT&CK Mobile technique is application-access-token theft and replay (Steal Application Access Token). The structural control is a short access-token TTL plus a server-revocable, rotating, device-bound refresh token (DPoP- or mTLS-style key custody in hardware), and a server-side revocation list the backend checks on every refresh — the missing server-side invariant is that the backend, not the device's local clock, decides whether a presented token is still alive.

**Pattern: On-device secrets, tokens, and cached PII are never wiped on logout, server-side deauthorization, or biometric-enrollment change.**

- Severity: high (reversibility and data-sensitivity load: a stolen or co-resident-app-readable device still holds live credentials and cached PII/PHI after the user logged out or the account was deauthorized server-side; the device-trust assumption is only the casual case, since unprotected leftovers survive a backup restore)
- NIST: SC-28, MP-6, SI-12, AC-12, IA-5
- Related concerns: Confidentiality (the at-rest protection of those secrets while they legitimately exist); routes server-side session/token invalidation and the deauthorization signal to api-security/identity-security
- Detail: This sits across MASVS-STORAGE-1 (the on-device secret/cache lifecycle) and MASVS-AUTH-2 (logout and deauthorization), in the MASWE storage-and-session-residue areas. The relevant MASTG work is the storage test group's check that sensitive data does not persist after logout and the AUTH check that local state is cleared on deauthorization, plus testing that biometric-enrollment change invalidates biometric-gated material. Closest CWE is CWE-459 Incomplete Cleanup. The ATT&CK Mobile technique is reading protected/unprotected app data and stored credentials on a controlled device (Access Stored Application Data). The structural control is a full client-side wipe on logout/deauth/biometric-change — clear tokens from Keychain/Keystore, evict cached PII from SharedPreferences, plist, NSUserDefaults, and SQLite, and invalidate biometric-gated keys on `BiometricPrompt`/`LAContext` enrollment change — but because the wipe runs on an attacker-controlled device, the finding of record is the missing server-side invariant: server-side session and token invalidation on logout/deauth so a device that fails to wipe still holds nothing the backend will honor.

**Pattern: Keystore/Keychain keys are never rotated and are not invalidated on credential change or biometric-enrollment change.**

- Severity: high (reversibility and data-sensitivity: a key that never rotates means one extraction or one stale biometric binding grants indefinite access to everything the key wraps, including stored tokens and cached PII; lowers toward medium only when the key is hardware-non-exportable and single-device blast radius holds)
- NIST: SC-12, SC-12(1), SC-28, IA-5, IA-5(2)
- Related concerns: Confidentiality (the key's at-rest custody and non-exportability); Authenticity (a key-attestation refresh proves the rotated key still lives in hardware) — the protocol decisions around server-side key registration route to identity-security
- Detail: This is the lifetime face of MASVS-CRYPTO-2 (the key-management area of MASWE under CRYPTO), distinct from the algorithm-strength facet. A key bound with `setInvalidatedByBiometricEnrollment` on Android, or with a Secure Enclave / StrongBox access control tied to current biometric set on iOS, should become unusable when a new fingerprint or face is enrolled; absent that, an attacker who enrolls their own biometric on a stolen device unlocks the user's gated secrets. The relevant MASTG work is the cryptography test group's key-management and key-lifecycle testing and the verification that biometric-gated keys invalidate on enrollment change. Closest CWE is CWE-324 Use of a Key Past its Expiration Date. The structural control is a defined key-rotation cadence plus automatic key-invalidation on credential or biometric-enrollment change, with re-derivation forcing re-authentication; the missing server-side invariant is that key rotation re-registers a fresh attested public key with the backend so a rotated client key is provably current and the prior key is server-rejected.

**Pattern: The attestation token / device-binding assertion has no freshness or TTL, so a once-valid verdict is replayable.**

- Severity: high (scale-vector and reversibility: a captured-once genuine-device verdict replayed indefinitely lets a rooted or emulated client masquerade as genuine fleet-wide, defeating the only cryptographic proof the request came from a real app on a real device; escalates to critical when the backend grants a privileged action on the strength of the verdict)
- NIST: IA-5, SC-12, AU-10, SI-7, SC-23
- Related concerns: Authenticity (the attestation verdict is the verifiable client identity); routes the backend's verdict-verification logic and nonce issuance to api-security, and any federation of the attestation signal to identity-security
- Detail: This is the freshness face of MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 (hardware-backed attestation), in the MASWE attestation area. A Play Integrity verdict or an App Attest assertion is only meaningful for the request it was minted for: without a server-issued nonce bound into the verdict and a tight TTL, a verdict captured on a genuine device is replayed from an instrumented one. The relevant MASTG work is the RESILIENCE test group's device-/app-attestation testing and the verification that the verdict is server-checked rather than client-trusted. Closest CWE is CWE-294 Authentication Bypass by Capture-replay. The ATT&CK Mobile technique context is evading the device-integrity check and replaying a captured attestation. The structural control is a server-issued challenge nonce embedded in every attestation request plus a short verdict TTL, and the missing server-side invariant is that the backend verifies verdict freshness and nonce binding on every consequential request — never caching or trusting a prior pass — so a stale or replayed verdict is rejected at the server, not merely re-requested on the client.

**Pattern: There is no forced-version sunset or kill-switch, so vulnerable client versions live on the install base indefinitely.**

- Severity: medium (scale-vector loads but the exposure is latent: without a sunset, a build with a known client weakness keeps running fleet-wide with no path to retire it; escalates to high or critical once a specific exploitable client CVE or a leaked embedded secret is live in a shipped version that cannot be forced off)
- NIST: CM-2, CM-3, SI-2, SI-7, CM-8
- Related concerns: Resilient (the graceful-degradation client path when a version is sunset — block-with-upgrade rather than crash); routes the server-side minimum-version gate and the kill-switch decision endpoint to api-security
- Detail: This is the Ephemeral-owned face of MASVS-CODE-4 (the outdated-/unsupported-version area of MASWE under CODE) — the lifetime of a client build, distinct from the dependency-currency facet CODE-3 owns. Without a forced minimum-supported-version sunset, store auto-update is the only retirement mechanism, and users who never update keep a vulnerable binary alive; obfuscation does not help, because the weakness ships in the bytes already on the device. The relevant MASTG work is the CODE test group's testing for an enforced minimum-version / update mechanism. Closest CWE is CWE-1104 Use of Unmaintained Third Party Components when the lingering version carries a known-vulnerable dependency. The structural control is a server-enforced minimum-supported-version gate plus a kill-switch for a specific bad build, and the missing server-side invariant is exactly that gate: the backend refuses to serve a client below the floor regardless of what the client claims, because a client-side version check on an adversary-controlled binary is trivially patched out and is not a control. In a NIST SP 800-163r1 managed-distribution context the sunset is also the enforcement arm of re-vetting: a new build must be re-vetted and re-approved before distribution, and the kill-switch retires versions that fail or predate that approval — so the minimum-supported-version floor is the organization's vetting decision made executable. The same minimum-version sunset must govern the hybrid-app OTA / code-push channel (CodePush, EAS Update): a server-enforced minimum-bundle-version gate has to reject a downgraded or un-re-vetted JavaScript bundle, or the OTA channel becomes a path to keep a vulnerable bundle alive — or push a fresh one — outside the store-review and version-gate the native binary is subject to.

**Pattern: A pinned certificate or embedded trust anchor has no expiry, rotation, or backup-pin lifecycle.**

- Severity: medium (operational-fragility and scale-vector: a pin set with no rotation path either expires into a fleet-wide self-inflicted outage when the cert rolls, or — if pinned to a long-lived anchor with no backup — cannot be rotated without an app update, leaving stale trust on the install base; rises with the value of the channel it guards)
- NIST: SC-12, SC-17, SC-23, CM-3, SI-7
- Related concerns: Confidentiality and Integrity (pinning protects the transport channel those goals own); Availability (an unrotatable pin is a self-DoS); routes the server-side certificate-rotation cadence and CA strategy to api-security
- Detail: This is the lifecycle face of MASVS-NETWORK-2 (certificate/public-key pinning), in the MASWE pinning area — distinct from the presence-of-pinning facet, which is an Integrity/Confidentiality concern. A pin embedded in the binary (Network Security Config on Android, App Transport Security plus pin material on iOS) is only as fresh as the last release; without backup pins and a documented rotation that ships ahead of the cert roll, the pinned set is a brittle, ephemeral-by-accident artifact. The relevant MASTG work is the NETWORK test group's pinning testing and the verification of a backup-pin and rotation strategy. Closest CWE is CWE-324 Use of a Key Past its Expiration Date applied to the pin material. The structural control is a pin-rotation lifecycle with backup pins, an expiry calendar aligned to certificate renewal, and an out-of-band update path; the missing server-side invariant is that the backend's certificate-rotation cadence is coordinated with the client's pin-update releases so the trust anchor is provably current on both ends rather than frozen in a shipped binary.

**Pattern: Tech plan asserts tokens, keys, attestation, or pinning generically without specifying lifetime, rotation cadence, wipe-on-deauth behavior, or version-sunset enforcement.**

- Disposition: blocked or uncertainty
- prerequisite_evidence: "Client credential-lifecycle policy — access/refresh-token TTL and the server-side revocation and device-binding mechanism; on-device wipe behavior on logout, server deauthorization, and biometric-enrollment change; Keystore/Keychain key-rotation cadence and key-invalidation-on-credential/biometric-change configuration (`setInvalidatedByBiometricEnrollment` / Secure Enclave access control); attestation-verdict nonce-binding and TTL with server-side freshness verification; the server-enforced minimum-supported-version gate and kill-switch behavior plus the graceful client path; and the certificate-pin rotation, backup-pin, and expiry strategy"

## Common capability patterns

**Pattern: Short-TTL access tokens paired with server-revocable, device-bound, rotating refresh tokens.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan asserts short access-token lifetimes and refresh rotation; implemented once the refresh-rotation flow and a server-side revocation/introspection check are wired and a device-bound key (DPoP- or mTLS-style custody in Keystore/Secure Enclave) is generated per device; tested when extracted-token replay and post-revocation replay are exercised against the backend and rejected; operationalized when refresh-token-reuse detection feeds a runbook that revokes the token family and the server-side revocation list is monitored. Cross-cuts Authenticity for the device-binding proof; the OAuth/OIDC token-issuance pipeline routes to identity-security via `related_concerns`.

**Pattern: Full on-device secret and cache wipe on logout, server deauthorization, and biometric-enrollment change.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan names the wipe trigger set; implemented once logout and the server deauthorization signal both clear tokens and cached PII from Keychain/Keystore, SharedPreferences, plist, NSUserDefaults, and SQLite, and biometric-gated keys are bound to invalidate on enrollment change; tested when a post-logout device image and a post-enrollment-change run confirm no live credential or cached PII remains and no backup restores one; operationalized when the wipe is paired with server-side session/token invalidation so a device that fails to wipe holds nothing the backend honors, with the deauth-to-wipe latency monitored.

**Pattern: Keystore/Keychain key rotation with automatic invalidation on credential or biometric-enrollment change.** Maturity: designed when a rotation cadence and the invalidation triggers are stated; implemented once keys are generated with `setInvalidatedByBiometricEnrollment` (Android) or a Secure Enclave / StrongBox access control bound to the current biometric set (iOS) and a rotation routine re-derives and re-wraps secrets; tested when an enrolled-attacker-biometric run on a controlled device confirms the gated key is unusable and rotation forces re-authentication; operationalized when each rotation re-registers a fresh attested public key with the backend and the prior key is server-rejected, with rotation events recorded in the server-side audit.

**Pattern: Server-enforced minimum-supported-version sunset with a graceful client upgrade path.** Maturity: designed when the tech plan asserts a minimum-version gate and a kill-switch; implemented once the backend refuses to serve clients below the floor (independently of any client-side claim) and the client renders a block-with-upgrade screen rather than crashing; tested when a below-floor build is confirmed server-rejected and the kill-switch retires a specific bad build fleet-wide in a drill; operationalized when the sunset and kill-switch events are logged server-side against signed-build provenance and the minimum-version floor is advanced on a cadence. Cross-cuts Resilient for the graceful-degradation client path; the server-side version gate routes to api-security via `related_concerns`.
