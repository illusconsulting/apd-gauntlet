# Mobile application common patterns — Non-Repudiation

These are illustrative templates, not all-inclusive. Use them to calibrate analytical style, severity assignment per the mobile application security rubric, and NIST/ATT&CK mapping habits. The specialist agent's analytical checklist still drives the actual analysis — this file calibrates how findings and capabilities should look once written.

## Common finding patterns

**Pattern: The audit trail for consequential actions exists only on the device — written to local SQLite, `SharedPreferences`, a file, or os_log/logcat — with no durable, server-anchored record of the action.**

- Severity: high (the mobile non-repudiation core: an on-device-only log is attacker-erasable on a rooted/jailbroken or stolen device — Magisk-rooted Android or a Frida-instrumented process can rewrite or delete it at will — so it is not a durable record but no record; the client-side-only-enforcement and reversibility modifiers both load, and the gap defeats breach reconstruction and dispute response for the entire consequential surface)
- NIST: AU-2, AU-3, AU-3(1), AU-4, AU-9, AU-12
- Related concerns: integrity (the on-device store is a mutable, untrusted substrate — the alteration-resistance of the authoritative copy is the Immutability lens); routes to api-security for the server-side audit pipeline that must record the backend action of record, and the backend authorization decision the record attributes
- Detail: this is the dominant mobile non-repudiation gap. MASVS-STORAGE and the MASWE insufficient-logging / sensitive-data-in-logs areas frame it; the MASTG techniques for inspecting local data stores and device logs (the local-storage and logging analysis methods, Android logcat and iOS os_log review) demonstrate that anything the app wrote on-device is reachable and editable by a privileged on-device attacker, which is the ATT&CK Mobile "Indicator Removal on Host" outcome — log deletion to repudiate an act. CWE-778 (insufficient logging) plus CWE-779 compound it. The structural control is that every consequential action enumerated in the consequential-action surface produces an authoritative server-side record; the device event is corroborating, never authoritative, and a device-originated action whose only trace is on the device is filed as an audit gap, not an audit.

**Pattern: Consequential backend transactions initiated from the device (funds movement, profile/credential mutation, sharing or permission changes, deletion) are logged server-side but without device, app-version, or attestation-verdict attribution.**

- Severity: high (the action is recorded but not attributable to the genuine-client context that performed it — a forged or repackaged client and a genuine one are indistinguishable in the record, defeating forged-client investigation and fraud reconstruction; the blast-radius modifier loads because the unattributed transaction surface pivots to the backend at fleet scale)
- NIST: AU-3, AU-3(1), AU-10, IA-3, IA-9
- Related concerns: authenticity (the attribution field is only as strong as the device-binding and attestation that populate it — weak client identity yields weak attribution); routes to api-security for the server-side recording of the action and to identity-security for the token/principal binding that names the user
- Detail: this is the AUTH-3 + RESILIENCE-4 attribution facet. MASVS-AUTH-3 (per-transaction step-up) and MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 (device/app integrity attestation) supply the signals; the MASWE insufficient-logging area and the MASTG techniques for verifying that integrity/attestation signals reach and are recorded by the backend frame the test. Without recording the Play Integrity / App Attest verdict, the app version, and the device-bound key identity (Android Keystore / iOS Keychain key) alongside each transaction, the record cannot answer "did a genuine app on a genuine device perform this" — the ATT&CK Mobile masquerading/repackaged-application path produces transactions indistinguishable from legitimate ones. The structural control is AU-3 content discipline at the backend: each consequential transaction record carries `actor_identifier` (user + device + app version + installation) and `attestation_verdict` per the audit-content fields, populated from server-verified signals rather than client assertions.

**Pattern: Tracking-consent and OS-permission grant and withdrawal are not durably recorded with scope, timestamp, app version, and the SDK set active at the time — there is no proof-of-consent fact base.**

- Severity: high (PRIVACY-3 proof: GDPR Article 7(1) requires the controller to demonstrate consent was given, and the App Tracking Transparency / consent-banner decision is the lawful-basis evidence for in-process SDK collection; absent a durable record the app cannot prove a withdrawal was honored or that collection was authorized, and the reversibility/data-sensitivity modifier loads on special-category and tracking data)
- NIST: AU-2, AU-3, AU-10, PT-1, PT-2
- Related concerns: immutability (withdrawal must be a new appended event, never an overwrite of the prior grant — the proof-of-prior-consent obligation outlives the consent, which is the Immutability retention lens); routes to api-security for the server-side consent-ledger pipeline
- Detail: this is the PRIVACY-3 record. MASVS-PRIVACY and the MASWE privacy/consent area frame it; the MASTG privacy-focused checks on what the app declares (privacy-manifest / Data-Safety) versus what it collects, and the App Tracking Transparency permission flow, are the testing reference. The consent fact must capture scope, timestamp, the app version, and the `sdk_collected_field` set live at consent time (per the data taxonomy), because consent is meaningful only relative to the SDKs that consumed it — a later SDK addition is a new in-process trust decision requiring fresh consent. The structural control is a server-anchored consent ledger that records each grant and withdrawal as an immutable event with full scope; the device toggle state is corroborating UI, not the proof of record.

**Pattern: Data-subject-rights requests — DSAR access, erasure, and export initiated from the app — are not recorded with the action taken and the records affected.**

- Severity: high (PRIVACY-4 demonstrability: GDPR Article 12 requires the controller to demonstrate that a rights request was received and acted on within the statutory window; without a request-and-disposition record the app cannot prove an erasure occurred or which records it touched, and the obligation survives the very data being erased)
- NIST: AU-2, AU-3, AU-10, PT-2, SI-12
- Related concerns: immutability (the request and its disposition remain immutable even after the underlying account data is erased — the pseudonymized record-of-deletion is the Immutability class); routes to api-security for the backend rights-fulfillment workflow and its audit
- Detail: this is the PRIVACY-4 record. MASVS-PRIVACY and the MASWE privacy area frame it; the request originates at a device UI but the authoritative record sits server-side. Each request must record receipt, the action taken (access fulfilled / erasure executed / export delivered), and the set of records affected — a record-of-deletion that persists as a pseudonymized fact after the account data is gone, so a regulator's demonstrability demand is answerable post-erasure. CWE-778 frames the missing-record gap. The structural control is a server-side rights-request ledger appended on receipt and on disposition; the device merely originates the request and must not be the only place the request is known to have happened.

**Pattern: Biometric step-up, biometric-gated key-release events, and backend attestation verdicts are unrecorded — there is no durable proof that the second factor was presented or that the genuine-client check ran.**

- Severity: high (cannot prove the AUTH-3 step-up actually occurred or that the RESILIENCE-4 attestation passed for a given high-value transaction; on a patched or Frida-hooked client a "step-up passed" or "attestation OK" assertion is forgeable, so only a server-recorded verdict is evidence — without it, dispute response cannot establish that a factor was ever presented, and the client-side-only-enforcement modifier loads)
- NIST: AU-3, AU-3(1), AU-10, IA-2(1), IA-3
- Related concerns: authenticity (the verdict's evidentiary value depends on the device-binding and attestation key that produced it); routes to api-security for the server-side recording of the step-up/verdict event and to identity-security for the step-up protocol and assurance-level binding
- Detail: this is the AUTH-3 + RESILIENCE-4 event-recording facet. The audit anchor for biometrics is the key-release event bound to the hardware authenticator — a `BiometricPrompt` / `LAContext` success that releases a biometric-gated Android Keystore / StrongBox or iOS Keychain / Secure Enclave key — not the prompt UI, which a hooked client can simulate. MASVS-AUTH-3 and MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 frame the signals; the MASTG techniques for testing local authentication and for verifying attestation reach the backend are the reference. A biometric-enrollment change (which the platform uses to invalidate biometric-gated keys) is itself an anchor event. The ATT&CK Mobile path is a defeated/bypassed local check presented to the backend as having passed. The structural control: the backend records the step-up and the Play Integrity / App Attest verdict (pass / fail / replay-or-stale detected) as its own server-anchored event per high-value transaction, never trusting a client-asserted flag.

**Pattern: Sensitive data — tokens, PII/PHI, key material — is captured into device logs, crash reports, or analytics breadcrumbs that feed the audit/telemetry trail, degrading the sensitivity class of the audit content (sensitivity inheritance).**

- Severity: high (audit storage inherits the highest sensitivity of any field captured per the data taxonomy — a logcat or os_log line, a crash report, or an analytics breadcrumb carrying a `stored_access_token` or `profile_pii` makes the whole telemetry sink token- or PII-sensitive, exposing it to co-resident apps reading logs, the crash-reporting SDK's egress, and device backups; the scale-vector loads when the offending log line ships fleet-wide via an analytics SDK)
- NIST: AU-9, SC-28, SI-11, AU-3
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the at-rest and in-transit protection of the now-elevated audit content is the Confidentiality lens); routes to api-security for the server-side log-ingest pipeline that must sustain the inherited sensitivity class
- Detail: MASVS-STORAGE-1 (no sensitive data in logs) and the MASWE sensitive-data-in-logs area frame it; the MASTG techniques for inspecting logcat / os_log and crash-report payloads are the test. CWE-532 (insertion of sensitive information into a log file) is the direct mapping. The non-repudiation concern is that the audit and telemetry surface, once it captures a secret, must be handled at the secret's sensitivity — access-controlled, retention-bound, and never the wider-read analytics tier — so the structural control is two-sided: scrub sensitive fields at the log/crash/analytics boundary (the Confidentiality fix), and where the field is legitimately part of the audit content, classify the audit sink to the inherited sensitivity and gate read access accordingly. A device-local crash reporter or analytics SDK that ships these breadcrumbs off-device is the ATT&CK Mobile data-from-local-system / exfiltration-over-third-party path.

**Pattern: In a managed/enterprise distribution context, app-vetting and approval decisions are not durably recorded — there is no server-anchored audit of which build was vetted, by what analysis, against which security requirements, and who approved it.**

- Severity: medium to high (the organization cannot demonstrate due diligence for a deployed app, cannot reconstruct why a version was approved or rejected, and cannot prove a vulnerable build was retired — the gap defeats the NIST SP 800-163r1 accountability the vetting process exists to provide; rises with the sensitivity of the data the deployed app handles)
- NIST: AU-2, AU-3, AU-12, CM-3, RA-3
- Related concerns: immutability (the vetting-decision record must be write-once — see the App-vetting decision class in immutability.md); authenticity (the decision is anchored to a verifiable build / signing identity); routes the vetting-system workflow and its audit to api-security
- Detail: this is the NIST SP 800-163r1 app-vetting lifecycle (planning → app testing → app approval/rejection) read as a consequential-action surface. The vetting process's value is accountability — that an organization tested an app and approved it against its security requirements. Without a durable, server-anchored record of the app submission, the static/dynamic/human analysis outcome and any reputation signal, the approval/rejection decision and the approver identity, and the re-vetting triggered by an update, the organization cannot defend a deployment decision or prove a rejected or vulnerable build was kept off the fleet. The structural control is a vetting-decision ledger recorded by the vetting system / backend per build and version, complementing — not replacing — the OWASP MASTG technical test evidence; the device is not a participant in this record. This pattern applies only where an organization vets/approves apps (managed/enterprise distribution); pure public-App-Store distribution has no such record to keep.

**Pattern: Tech plan asserts "all consequential actions are audited" or "events are sent to the backend" generically without specifying which action classes are covered, what attribution fields each record carries, or that the sink is server-anchored rather than device-local.**

- Disposition: blocked or uncertainty
- prerequisite_evidence: "Per-action-class audit specification — the enumerated consequential-action classes covered (against the consequential-action surface), the audit-content fields per class (actor_identifier including user/device/app-version/installation, action, resource, attestation_verdict, decision_outcome, request_metadata), confirmation that the authoritative record is server-anchored and not on-device-only, the device-attribution and attestation-verdict fields carried on backend-transaction records, and the consent/DSAR ledger schema with scope/timestamp/app-version/SDK-set"

## Common capability patterns

**Pattern: Server-anchored per-action audit across the consequential surface, each record carrying device, app-version, and attestation-verdict attribution.** Scope must enumerate the covered action classes against the consequential-action surface — authentication/session events, consequential backend transactions, sensitive-data access/export, deep-link/IPC/push-initiated actions. Maturity: designed when a tech plan names the server-side sink and the per-class attribution schema (actor including user/device/app-version/installation, action, resource, attestation verdict, decision outcome); implemented when backend records are emitted per consequential transaction with the device and app-version fields populated from server-verified signals; tested when the device-attribution and attestation fields are confirmed present and correct on real backend records (and a forged/repackaged client is shown to be distinguishable in the trail); operationalized when coverage is monitored against the consequential-action surface, on-device logs are confirmed corroborating-only, and out-of-evidence action classes are caveated.

**Pattern: Durable, server-anchored consent and tracking-permission records capturing scope, timestamp, app version, and the active SDK set, with withdrawal recorded as a new appended event.** Maturity: designed when the consent-ledger schema is specified (scope, timestamp, app version, `sdk_collected_field` set at consent time, grant and withdrawal events) and consent is declared a server-anchored fact; implemented when each App Tracking Transparency / consent-banner decision and OS-permission grant/withdrawal is appended to the server ledger; tested when a withdrawal is shown to produce a new immutable event (never an overwrite) and the recorded SDK set matches the SDKs actually live at consent time; operationalized when proof-of-consent can be produced on demand for an arbitrary user-and-timepoint, and a new SDK addition is shown to force a fresh consent decision.

**Pattern: Data-subject-rights request-and-response records — DSAR access, erasure, export — capturing receipt, action taken, and records affected.** Maturity: designed when the rights-request ledger schema is specified, including the pseudonymized record-of-deletion that survives account erasure; implemented when a request originating from the app appends a receipt record and a disposition record at fulfillment; tested when an erasure is shown to leave an immutable record of what was deleted after the underlying data is gone, and the affected-record set is captured; operationalized when GDPR Article 12 demonstrability can be produced post-erasure for an arbitrary request, and the device is confirmed to merely originate the request rather than being its only record.

**Pattern: Step-up and attestation-verdict events recorded server-side per high-value transaction.** Maturity: designed when the backend specifies recording the AUTH-3 step-up event and the Play Integrity / App Attest verdict (pass / fail / replay-or-stale) as its own server-anchored events, with the biometric anchor identified as the key-release event rather than the prompt; implemented when each high-value transaction emits a server-side step-up and verdict record bound to the device key identity; tested when the record is confirmed to derive from a server-verified verdict — not a client-asserted "passed" flag — and a hooked client asserting success is shown to produce no passing verdict; operationalized when biometric-enrollment-change invalidations and pinning-validation failures are also captured as anchor events, and the verdict trail can reconstruct, for any disputed transaction, whether a genuine second factor and a genuine-client check occurred.
