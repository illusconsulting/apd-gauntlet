# Mobile application common patterns — Immutability

These are illustrative templates, not all-inclusive. Use them to calibrate analytical style, severity assignment per the mobile application security rubric, and NIST/ATT&CK mapping habits. The specialist agent's analytical checklist still drives the actual analysis — this file calibrates how findings and capabilities should look once written.

The eight MASVS categories are cross-cutting, not goals: no MASVS category maps to a single APD goal, and Immutability owns only the write-once-protection facet of the privacy, resilience, and auth records below. And client-side enforcement of a server-side control is not a control: the device is not a tamper-evident substrate. A rooted device, a Frida-instrumented process, or a restored backup can rewrite anything the app can write, so the authoritative immutable copy is always SERVER-SIDE / WORM — an on-device record is at most corroborating, never the record of obligation. Every pattern below names the missing server-side immutable invariant as the finding of record.

## Common finding patterns

**Pattern: Consent / tracking-permission records held on a mutable store or device-only — back-editable, so the proof-of-lawful-basis is destroyed.**

- Severity: high (reversibility / data-sensitivity loads — proof-of-prior-consent is a legal obligation that outlives the consent itself, and a record only on the device is attacker-erasable on a rooted or restored device; rises to critical when merged with a Non-Repudiation gap on the same PRIVACY-3 class under the GDPR accountability anchor)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-9(2), AU-9(3), AU-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: non_repudiation (whether the consent grant/withdrawal events exist and carry trustworthy attribution); confidentiality (encryption-at-rest of the consent store). Server-side consent-store enforcement routes to api-security; OAuth/OIDC consent-grant protocol semantics route to identity-security.
- Detail: This is the MASVS-PRIVACY-3 proof-of-consent — the App Tracking Transparency / consent-banner decision, the active SDK set at the time of consent, and the withdrawal record — expressed as a tamper-erasable obligation; the MASWE privacy-and-consent weakness area covers a consent state that is mutable or recorded only client-side, and MASTG privacy testing exercises whether the recorded consent matches actual collection. CWE-778 (insufficient logging) and CWE-volatile-storage of an obligation apply. The ATT&CK Mobile indicator-removal-on-device technique is the on-device erasure path. The structural control is a server-side WORM / append-only consent ledger where withdrawal is written as a new event that never overwrites the prior grant (GDPR Article 6(1)(a) / Article 7 lawful-basis evidence); the device's `tracking_permission_state` is mutable and untrusted, and the durable record of obligation lives on the backend with retention pinned to the longest of the GDPR documentation duration and any app-store policy floor.

**Pattern: DSAR / erasure / export request-and-response records mutable — GDPR Article 12 demonstrability defeated.**

- Severity: high (reversibility / data-sensitivity loads — the demonstrability of having honored a data-subject right is itself the evidence, and it must survive the erasure of the underlying account data; back-editable disposition records mean the operator cannot prove a request was honored)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-9(2), AU-9(3), AU-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: non_repudiation (completeness and attribution of the DSAR receipt/disposition events); confidentiality (the pseudonymized record-of-deletion must not re-expose the erased data). Server-side DSAR-workflow enforcement and the erasure of backend datastores route to api-security.
- Detail: This is the MASVS-PRIVACY-4 data-subject-rights record — receipt, action taken, and the records affected for access / erasure / export — and the MASWE privacy weakness area covers a mobile flow whose only record of a DSAR is a mutable on-device or back-editable backend row. MASTG privacy testing checks whether the app's declared data handling is demonstrable. CWE-778 and tamperable-audit weaknesses apply; the on-device erasure of the disposition record maps to the ATT&CK Mobile indicator-removal technique. The structural control is a server-side immutable DSAR ledger holding a pseudonymized record-of-deletion that remains immutable even after the account data is erased, so the request and its disposition satisfy GDPR Article 12 demonstrability after the fact; the device representation is corroborating only.

**Pattern: Signed-build / app-signing provenance plus SBOM not version-historied or immutable — repackaging attribution is lost.**

- Severity: high (scale-vector loads — the link between a store version and its provenance is what answers "is this the build we shipped?" across the entire install base; without an immutable build digest / signing-identity / SBOM record, a repackaged or backdoored build cannot be distinguished from the genuine one and the forced-update / kill-switch decision has no trustworthy baseline)
- NIST: SR-4, SR-4(3), CM-8, AU-9, AU-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: authenticity (the app-signing key as the trust root behind the provenance; APK Signature Scheme v2+/v3 and iOS code-signing identity); resilient (the forced-update / kill-switch decision the provenance underwrites); non_repudiation (which signing identity produced which build). Backend SBOM-attestation storage and the SLSA provenance chain route to api-security.
- Detail: This is MASVS-RESILIENCE-3 (anti-repackaging / tamper attribution) coupled to MASVS-CODE-1/CODE-2 (the build's dependency and signing posture), and the MASWE code-quality and resilience weakness areas cover an app whose provenance is not retained per release; MASTG reverse-engineering and code-tampering techniques exercise repackaging and re-signing (a Magisk-rooted device or a re-signed APK side-loaded past App Links). CWE-1104 (use of unmaintained third-party components) and CWE-345 (insufficient verification of authenticity) apply, and the distribute-malicious-app / repackaged-application ATT&CK Mobile technique is the threat. The structural control is a signed, version-historied build-provenance record — build digest, signing identity, SBOM, release version — held immutable server-side so the link between any store/side-loaded binary and its claimed provenance is verifiable and the kill-switch can act on a trustworthy "this is not the build we shipped" determination.

**Pattern: Attestation-verification records mutable — cannot prove which device and app version presented which verdict at time T.**

- Severity: high (reversibility / data-sensitivity loads — the attestation audit chain is the only basis for reconstructing a forged-client investigation after the fact; a mutable verdict record lets the fact that a stale or forged verdict was accepted be edited away)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-9(2), AU-9(3), AU-10, AU-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: authenticity (the Play Integrity / App Attest verdict as the verifiable client identity); non_repudiation (completeness of the accept/reject decision record); ephemeral (verdict freshness — a stale verdict is replayable). Server-side verdict verification and nonce-freshness enforcement route to api-security; the attestation-authority protocol routes to identity-security.
- Detail: This is the MASVS-RESILIENCE-4 hardware-attestation record — which device and app version presented which Play Integrity / App Attest verdict at time T and the backend's accept/reject outcome — and the MASWE resilience weakness area covers a backend that consumes attestation but does not retain a tamper-evident record of the verdicts it saw; MASTG resilience testing exercises attestation bypass and verdict replay. CWE-345 (insufficient verification of data authenticity) and CWE-294 (authentication bypass by capture-replay) apply, and the ATT&CK Mobile indicator-removal and forged-attestation techniques are the threats. The structural control is a server-anchored, append-only attestation-verification ledger (the verdict, the device/app-version binding, the nonce, and the decision) on a WORM substrate, with retention pinned to the incident-reconstruction window so a forged-client investigation can be rebuilt; the client-presented verdict is untrusted input, and the immutable record is the backend's record of what it accepted.

**Pattern: Authentication / token-revocation / tamper-signal records mutable or held device-only.**

- Severity: high (blast-radius and reversibility load — the stolen-device response depends on a provable revocation event, and credential-compromise reconstruction depends on a tamper-evident server-side authn record; a revocation or sign-in record that is mutable or only on the device cannot anchor an incident timeline; rises to critical when merged with a Non-Repudiation completeness gap on the same AUTH class)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-9(2), AU-9(3), AU-10, AU-11, SI-7
- Related concerns: non_repudiation (existence and attribution of the sign-in / step-up / revocation events); ephemeral (the revocation event is what bounds a long-lived token's window); authenticity (the identity bound to each event). Server-side session/token revocation and OAuth/OIDC revocation-endpoint semantics route to identity-security; backend authn-log enforcement routes to api-security.
- Detail: This is the MASVS-AUTH-1/AUTH-2 event base — interactive sign-in success/failure, step-up (the AUTH-3 anchor), token issuance/refresh/rotation, refresh-token-reuse detection, and the server-side token invalidation that logout must trigger — together with the root/jailbreak/Frida/repackaging tamper signals that must be shipped to and recorded by the server as advisory facts. The MASWE authentication weakness area covers a token with no server-side revocation record, and MASTG authentication testing exercises token replay after logout. CWE-613 (insufficient session expiration) and CWE-778 apply; the on-device clearing of a tamper signal maps to the ATT&CK Mobile indicator-removal technique. The structural control is a server-side, tamper-evident authn / revocation / tamper-signal ledger — the device clearing local state is not revocation, and the durable record of "token issued at T, revoked at T′" must live server-side with retention matching or exceeding the longest breach-detection window plus the longest token lifetime issued.

**Pattern: SDK-collection / data-flow records not retained immutably for Data-Safety / privacy-manifest attestation defensibility.**

- Severity: high (scale-vector loads — one in-process SDK across the install base, and the only defensible answer to a regulator's or app store's "does the declaration match reality?" is an immutable record of which SDK version collected which fields and shipped them where; a mutable record lets a divergence between declared and actual collection be edited to match the declaration after the fact)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-9(2), AU-11, SI-7, SR-4
- Related concerns: confidentiality (the SDK egress is also a covert in-process exfiltration channel); non_repudiation (per-SDK collection and egress events as the privacy fact base); authenticity (SDK version / provenance as a new in-process trust decision per build). Backend storage of the collection ledger routes to api-security.
- Detail: This is the MASVS-PRIVACY-1/PRIVACY-2 fact base — the per-SDK `sdk_collected_field` set, the network egress destination, and the SDK version change that is a new trust decision — held against the iOS privacy-manifest declaration and the Google Play Data Safety declaration. The MASWE privacy weakness area covers an app that cannot demonstrate its declaration matched its in-process behavior, and MASTG privacy testing inspects SDK data flows against the declared manifest. CWE-359 (exposure of private information) and tamperable-record weaknesses apply; on-device alteration maps to the ATT&CK Mobile indicator-removal technique. The structural control is a server-side immutable SDK-collection / data-flow ledger (SDK version, fields read, egress destination, time) retained for privacy-manifest and Data-Safety attestation defensibility, because both an app store and a regulator can demand proof the declaration matched reality.

**Pattern: App-vetting decision and analysis-report records are mutable or absent — the organization cannot prove which build it approved, against which security requirements, at time T.**

- Severity: medium to high (in a managed/enterprise distribution context the vetting decision is the organization's due-diligence evidence; a back-editable approval record lets a deployment decision be rewritten after an incident, and a new build's re-vetting overwriting the prior decision destroys the history the NIST SP 800-163r1 process exists to create)
- NIST: AU-9, AU-11, CM-3, CM-8, SI-7
- Related concerns: non_repudiation (existence and attribution of the vetting/approval decision events — see the vetting-audit pattern in non-repudiation.md); authenticity (the decision binds to a verifiable signed-build identity). The vetting-system workflow and storage route to api-security.
- Detail: This is the immutability face of the NIST SP 800-163r1 app-vetting lifecycle. The vetting report (static/dynamic/human analysis), the organization-defined security-requirements baseline applied, the reputation signal, and the approve/reject decision with its approver identity must be retained immutably, bound to the exact build/version vetted, so the organization can demonstrate which version it approved against which requirements and reconstruct or defend the decision after the fact. Re-vetting on an update is a new immutable decision, never an overwrite of the prior one. CWE-778 and tamperable-record weaknesses apply. The structural control is a server-side WORM / append-only vetting ledger with retention pinned to the longest of the organization's audit-retention policy and the deployment lifetime of any version the decision authorized; the device plays no part in this record. Applies only to managed/enterprise distribution where an organization vets apps before deployment.

**Pattern: Tech plan describes retaining consent, DSAR, attestation, and provenance records generically without specifying the WORM substrate, lock mode, retention floor, and credential boundary.**

- Disposition: blocked or uncertainty
- prerequisite_evidence: "Immutability / retention policy per record class (consent PRIVACY-3, DSAR PRIVACY-4, attestation RESILIENCE-4, build provenance + SBOM RESILIENCE-3/CODE-1/2, authn + revocation AUTH-1/2, SDK-collection PRIVACY-1/2) — the WORM / object-lock substrate and lock mode (compliance vs governance), the retention floor pinned to the longest applicable regulatory or app-store or incident-reconstruction window with its citation, the hash-chain head anchoring location for any append-only log, the legal-hold override procedure, and the explicit credential boundary proving no single device or app credential can downgrade, delete, or alter the immutable tier"

## Common capability patterns

**Pattern: Server-side WORM / object-lock for consent, DSAR, attestation, and provenance records, with retention pinned to the longest applicable regulatory or store floor.** Maturity ladder: designed from a tech plan that names the WORM substrate and the lock mode per record class; implemented once object-lock (compliance mode) is configured on the consent / DSAR / attestation / provenance buckets and the retention period is set; tested once a write-over and an early-delete attempt are shown to be rejected and the retention floor is verified against the cited regulatory / app-store window; operationalized once the multi-regulator retention-pinning is evidenced enumerating every applicable floor (GDPR documentation duration, app-store policy floor, incident-reconstruction window) and pinning to the longest, with the device copies treated as mutable and untrusted.

**Pattern: Signed, version-historied build provenance plus SBOM linking source to the binary in the store.** Maturity ladder: designed from a release-pipeline plan that retains provenance per release; implemented once the build digest, signing identity (APK Signature Scheme v2+/v3, iOS code-signing identity), SBOM, and release version are recorded immutably per release; tested once a repackaged or re-signed binary is demonstrably distinguishable from the genuine build via the provenance record; operationalized once the forced-update / kill-switch decision provably consults the immutable provenance and the SBOM history is queryable per shipped version.

**Pattern: Revocation records proving a token was revoked at time T.** Maturity ladder: designed from an authn-logging plan that records issuance and revocation as distinct durable events; implemented once "token issued at T, revoked at T′" is written server-side on a tamper-evident substrate with the actor and device binding; tested once a post-logout / post-revocation replay is shown to be rejected and the revocation event is shown present and immutable; operationalized once revocation retention is pinned to the longest breach-detection window plus the longest token lifetime and the stolen-device response provably anchors to the revocation record rather than to the device clearing local state.

**Pattern: No single device or app credential able to alter the immutable tier.** Maturity ladder: designed from a least-privilege plan separating the write path to the immutable tier from any client-reachable credential; implemented once the object-lock / vault-lock is in compliance mode and no app or device credential holds the lock-downgrade, bypass-governance, or delete permission; tested once an attempt to alter or delete a locked record using a client-presented or app-service credential is shown to fail; operationalized once the credential boundary is audited — proving that a rooted device, an extracted app credential, or a compromised in-process SDK has no write path to the consent / DSAR / attestation / provenance / revocation ledgers.
